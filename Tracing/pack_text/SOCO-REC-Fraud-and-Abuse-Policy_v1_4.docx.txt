1. Purpose and scope
This policy protects four parties. Buyers should see genuine recommendations. Honest promoters should not be outranked by fake engagement. Sellers pay for real sales. Promoters earn commission that stores pay them directly, and SOCO records each commission and can reject it.
In scope: every account type (users, promoters and businesses). Commerce fraud, engagement fraud, and account fraud (multiple, shared or replacement accounts).
Out of scope: payment card fraud and chargebacks, which Razorpay handles. Account takeover and login security, which the platform's security layer handles. Content moderation for reasons other than fraud.
2. Principles
The system flags; a person decides. No account is restricted automatically in Wave 1A.
Proportionate. The response matches the severity and the history. A first minor case gets a warning.
Evidence first. No account is restricted without a reviewer recording the evidence.
No commission is rejected while in doubt. Commission is rejected only on orders confirmed as fraudulent. The platform has no hold, so commission under review can still be approved and paid (section 7).
Transparent to the person. Anyone restricted is told the reason category and how to appeal. Rules and thresholds are never disclosed.
Recorded. Every flag, decision and threshold in force is logged.
Calibrated. Thresholds are loosened where they produce too many false alarms.
3. Detection-only mode, until the roles are named
Until the policy owner and reviewer are named on the first page:
The engine runs every rule, records every hit, and opens cases at levels 1 and 2 as normal.
No warning, restriction, suspension or removal is applied to any account, and no notice is sent.
Cases stay open. None is ever acted on by default.
One rule applies anyway, because it is not a penalty: self-purchases never create commission (section 7). The platform already applies it, across every account a person owns.
Once the owner and reviewer are named, open cases are reviewed in order of priority, oldest first.

5. How fraud is detected in Wave 1A
The engine runs these rules nightly. A hit is a signal, never a verdict. Every threshold is provisional, is read from configuration, and is logged with every hit. Incognito orders are included, otherwise incognito would be a way around R03 and R05. The engine reads every relevant record: actions later undone (an unlike, an unfollow), actions on a person's own posts (the platform's is_self flag is a supporting signal only, never evidence on its own), and cancelled, rejected, returned and incognito orders. Order outcomes are read from the order itself: order_status, with its history in tracking_info. A return is an order whose order_status is Returned, which the seller sets when the return is recorded. There is no Return Requested status. A record read again by the engine is the same evidence, never a new offence.

7. Commission and earnings
Three different rules touch commission, and they are kept apart. Platform commission, as the platform pays it: a commission is approved once the order has been Delivered for the longer of the hold days (7 by default) and the product's return window plus 2 days. A return request does not pause it. Cancelled, rejected or returned orders never earn commission: if an order becomes Cancelled, Rejected or Returned, the commission is reversed, and if it was already paid, the platform records a clawback from the promoter's next payout from that store. This applies to everyone and is not a penalty.
Recommendation measurement, not a commission rule: the engine's promoter score counts a sale as completed only when, in addition, no return request is open (build list ENG-16). It is stricter than the platform on purpose, and it does not change what anyone is paid.
Fraud enforcement: commission is rejected only on a confirmed case, by an admin with the role to do it, as set out below. A rule hit or an open case alone never rejects commission.
Self-purchases never create commission. A person's personal account and every store they own count as one buyer: when every creator on the post is the buyer's own account, no commission is created. The platform applies this from launch. Three or more in 30 days opens a review under R03.
Stores pay commission to promoters directly, outside SOCO, and record the payment in SOCO. SOCO records every commission, and an admin with the super_admin, admin or finance_manager role can reject one while it is pending or approved. Rejection is final.
There is no hold. The platform cannot pause a commission while a case is open: pending commission is approved automatically once its approval date passes. When a case is confirmed, the commission on the offending orders is rejected through the admin reject; this is the void. Commission already paid cannot be rejected through SOCO, and the case record notes it.
If an appeal succeeds after a commission was rejected, the platform cannot restore it. SOCO arranges the payment with the store outside SOCO, and the case record notes it.
Before enforcement starts (once the roles are named), SOCO decides whether a commission hold is needed: a review can take longer than the 7-day hold window, so commission could be paid before a case is decided.
8. The review process
One reviewer, with a capacity of 20 cases a week. Levels 4 and 5 also need the second approver.
A reviewer never reviews a case involving someone they know personally. It passes to the second approver.
The review screen shows every rule hit with its value and the threshold in force, the orders, posts and accounts involved, earlier strikes and account age.
Confirmed needs at least one recorded fact that supports the hit and is independent of it. For example: the buyer and promoter share a device, the buyer is the promoter, the followers were all created on the same day with no other activity, or the person admits it. A rule hit alone is never enough.
Outcomes: confirmed, with a fraud code; false alarm (no strike, nothing rejected); or unclear (no strike, nothing rejected, back to Watch for 30 days).
An overdue case goes to the policy owner. It is never acted on by default.
The reviewer, or whoever rejects commission on a confirmed case, must hold an admin role that can reject commission (super_admin, admin or finance_manager).

An appeal can be made within 30 days of a notice, from the notice itself.
It is decided by the second approver, never the original reviewer, within 10 working days. The decision is final.
An overturned case removes the strike, lifts the restriction, arranges payment of rejected commission outside SOCO (section 7), and restores the promoter score at the next refresh.
10. Calibration and the false-alarm limit
Calibration starts once reviews exist. Every fortnight the engine compares each rule's reviewed cases with their outcomes.
If more than half of a rule's reviewed cases in a fortnight are false alarms, the rule is switched to watch-only, and the change is logged with the numbers behind it.
After 8 weeks of reviews, each rule's threshold is set to the value that kept false alarms at or below half. This is a fixed rule applied by the engine owner. It needs no approval.
Automatic restriction is not allowed in Wave 1A. Later, one rule may be allowed to restrict automatically, but only after it has shown at least 90% confirmed cases over at least 100 reviews.
11. Exemptions
Internal staff and test accounts go on an exemption list, each entry with a reason and an end date. Exempt accounts are still flagged and recorded, but never actioned. There is no exemption for new accounts, business accounts or high-earning promoters. The list is kept in the engine's settings: each entry has the account, the reason and an end date, and every change is logged. Test accounts are also on a separate list that is left out of recommendations learning and measurement.
12. Ownership and changes
Thresholds change only under the section 10 rule. Every change is logged with its date, the old and new value, and the evidence.
Every case records the policy version and the threshold values in force at that moment.
In an emergency, such as an active attack, the policy owner may tighten a threshold straight away, recording the reason in writing within 24 hours.
The policy is reviewed at the end of the 8-week calibration, then every quarter.
13. Data protection and retention
Device, network and login data are used only for fraud detection. They are never used for recommendations, never passed raw to any outside service, and stored as hashed fingerprints rather than raw IP addresses.
Case records are kept for 2 years after a case closes. Rule hits that never became a case are kept for 12 months.
Access is limited to the reviewer, the policy owner, the second approver and the engine.
Rule inputs come from the engine's copies of platform data, kept for at least 365 days. Keeping those copies does not change the case and rule-hit periods above, or who may see them.
Purchase privacy for incognito orders does not change fraud detection, and does not allow a buyer's identity to be shown to anyone outside the roles above.
How long records are kept after an account is deleted is one of the points for the advised legal review. Until then, deleting an account purges its recommendation data from the engine, but not its fraud case records, which follow the periods above.
